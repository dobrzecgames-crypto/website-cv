# Storyboard — website

This document describes the intended narrative flow. It is a direction, not a pixel-perfect implementation spec.

## Scene 00 — Arrival / finished object

### Viewer sees

Station first as a complete, coherent product.

The first impression should not be a wall of explanation. Let the finished object carry visual authority before explaining it.

### Purpose

Establish the end result before decomposition begins.

### Possible motif setup

Introduce one restrained visual object — for example a line, signal, marker or subtle light — without immediately explaining its future role.

Do not reveal every metaphor at once.

---

## Scene 01 — LASER / decomposition

### Trigger

A deliberate interaction such as **LASER IT**.

The user chooses to cut Station open rather than the page automatically performing its biggest effect without consent.

### Transformation

- slicing markers appear
- one short laser flash marks the cut
- Station separates into meaningful visual layers / chops
- those chops become the entry point to the deeper story

### Purpose

Change the viewer's role from "looking at a finished product" to "inspecting how it is built".

### Important

The pieces should correspond to real narrative categories. Do not cut the screenshot into arbitrary strips solely for visual effect.

---

## Scene 02 — PADS / material and interaction

### Transition idea

The LASER-created pieces begin to organize into a tactile or grid-like arrangement. The transition can suggest the logic of pads without becoming a literal clone of the Station PADS screen.

### Content goals

Show examples of:

- sampling / triggered material
- direct manipulation
- touch-first interaction thinking
- pad feel and physicality work
- gesture and control behavior
- practical UI problems solved during iteration

### Tone

Immediate, tactile, responsive.

### Narrative role

The cut pieces are no longer just fragments; they become playable material.

---

## Scene 03 — SYNTH / generation and transformation

### Transition idea

Grid/material logic begins to transform into signal logic: waveform, wavetable, modulation, envelope, routing or controlled morphing.

### Signature payoff candidate

A line introduced earlier as a simple visual element can reveal itself here as a waveform or wavetable.

The viewer should have a brief "that was this all along" realization.

### Content goals

Show selected instruments and systems rather than every control.

Potential subjects include:

- ZOLA-X
- other synth architecture worth showing
- custom browser-based synthesis
- modulation / sound design systems
- Web Audio as an engine rather than a novelty
- custom UI controls built specifically for musical interaction

### Tone

Exploratory but controlled. Avoid generic neon sci-fi treatment.

---

## Scene 04 — SEQ / SONG / hierarchy

### Transition idea

The fragments and signals begin to acquire position, order, grouping and time.

Elements that were previously free or separated start to align into a coherent structure.

### Content goals

Show:

- sequencing
- pattern / song organization
- timing and ordered state
- how many independent systems become one workflow
- product-level hierarchy rather than isolated features

### Narrative role

This is the beginning of reconstruction.

LASER decomposed Station. SEQ / SONG starts making the parts belong together again.

### Visual principle

This section can be quieter than LASER and SYNTH. The payoff is order itself.

---

## Scene 05 — MIX / judgment, iteration, destruction

### Core idea

MIX represents decision-making, not simply audio levels.

By this point the viewer has seen how much was built. Now show that building also required removing, reversing and simplifying.

### Possible visual metaphor

Treat product ideas as mixer channels.

Some channels remain strong. Others are reduced, muted or removed because the total system becomes better without them.

The master can approach overload/clipping when too much accumulates. Reducing unnecessary channels creates headroom again.

### Real examples to document

Current examples worth using:

- STRINGS was removed
- autotune was removed
- GRAVITY returned to the clearer SIDECHAIN naming
- visual concepts were repeatedly rebuilt when they felt too dashboard-like, too flat or too decorative

Verify final wording against Station history before publication.

### Early-version reveal

This is the first strong place to reveal older screenshots recovered from GitHub.

Do not show the primitive beginning too early. The viewer should know the final product first.

Possible structure:

- early build
- intermediate decisions
- final direction

The purpose is not "look how ugly this was". The purpose is to show iteration and judgment.

### Key message

**Not everything that works belongs in the final product.**

---

## Scene 06 — MASTER / final product

### Transformation

All surviving systems resolve into one coherent Station again.

The page should become calmer.

The final product can resemble the opening view, but the viewer now understands what sits inside it and what was removed to get there.

### Content goals

- final Station presentation
- concise final product statement
- live product link
- source / GitHub link where appropriate
- selected final technical facts only if they strengthen the conclusion

### Emotional role

Resolution rather than spectacle.

The story started with a finished object that was unexplained. It ends with a finished object that has been earned.

---

## Scene 07 — Other work

A short, visually clean secondary section may mention SkateRoad.

Possible contents:

- one strong image / short media element
- one concise explanation
- official project link
- itch.io link

Do not force SkateRoad into Station's LASER/PADS/SYNTH metaphor.

Its role is simply to demonstrate range beyond the flagship project.

---

## Scene 08 — Contact / exit

Keep the ending simple.

After MASTER, avoid restarting the spectacle. Provide direct routes to contact, GitHub and relevant work.

If an earlier motif is naturally capable of a quiet final resolution, it may close here. Do not force a callback merely because one is possible.

---

# Global pacing

The site should alternate between:

- **quiet comprehension**
- **meaningful transformation**
- **quiet comprehension**

Do not make every viewport a reveal.

A useful target is only a small number of genuinely memorable signature moments across the full experience.

# Interaction rule

Desktop may use richer pointer/scroll behavior, but no critical information may depend on hover or precision mouse control.

Mobile should be designed as a first-class narrative variant, not a compressed desktop animation.
