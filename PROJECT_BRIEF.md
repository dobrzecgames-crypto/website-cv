# Project Brief — website

## Purpose

`website` is a personal interactive website that must work as a product in its own right, not merely as a container for links to other projects.

Its job is to answer two questions before a recruiter or collaborator needs to ask them:

1. **Can this person build a polished, modern, responsive website or web application?**
2. **Can this person go materially beyond ordinary website work when the problem becomes unusual?**

The website itself answers the first question. Station answers the second.

## Primary audience

- people hiring for frontend / product / creative-development work
- teams building websites, dashboards, internal tools or interactive web products
- technical reviewers who may inspect implementation quality after seeing the finished experience
- creative teams interested in product thinking, interaction design and AI-assisted development

The site should remain understandable to a non-musician. Station's music-production concepts are used as storytelling devices, not as prerequisites.

## Main product story

Station is the flagship case study and should occupy the large majority of the experience.

The website follows the conceptual order of Station itself:

**LASER → PADS → SYNTH → SEQ / SONG → MIX → MASTER**

This is not a navigation gimmick. Each chapter represents a different creative/product operation:

- **LASER — decomposition.** Start with Station as one finished object, then cut it apart to inspect what is inside.
- **PADS — material and interaction.** Show tactile interaction, triggering, sampling workflow and direct manipulation.
- **SYNTH — generation and transformation.** Show instruments, sound generation, custom controls and the deeper capabilities of the browser.
- **SEQ / SONG — hierarchy and structure.** Previously separated elements begin to align, group and form a coherent system again.
- **MIX — judgment and reduction.** Show iteration, trade-offs, features and ideas that were changed, renamed, simplified or removed.
- **MASTER — resolution.** Reassemble the system into the finished Station product and present the final live result.

## Core narrative rule

**Do not move between major sections as unrelated slides. Transform one state into the next.**

If an object, line, light, grid or motion is introduced, it should have a reason to exist and, where useful, a later payoff. Example: a line may initially read as a simple graphic element and only later reveal itself to be a waveform or wavetable.

This does not mean every object must be reused. Avoid forced cleverness. A few strong setup/payoff moments are better than constant spectacle.

## What the site must demonstrate

Even if Station were removed, the website should still demonstrate professional web competence:

- responsive composition and typography
- clear information hierarchy
- modern CSS layout
- robust desktop and mobile behavior
- thoughtful interaction states
- keyboard and touch usability
- accessibility fundamentals
- high perceived visual quality without excessive runtime cost
- disciplined motion design
- good loading behavior and media handling
- maintainable component/system thinking

Station then demonstrates depth beyond ordinary frontend work: complex state, custom interaction, browser audio, musical systems, persistence, sequencing, synthesis and product iteration.

## Creative direction

The website should feel authored rather than templated. It must not look like:

- a generic developer portfolio template
- a copy of Apple product pages
- a SaaS dashboard
- an AI-generated dark-gradient landing page
- a synthwave cliché made of neon palms, retro sunsets and decorative grids

Station may influence the site's interaction language, but the website should not visually become another Station screen.

Use concepts from music technology abstractly: signal, waveform, slicing, timing, hierarchy, routing, level, reduction, mix, resolution.

## Product philosophy

The implementation should pursue **high perceived complexity with low unnecessary technical complexity**.

Use the browser's native capabilities aggressively before adding dependencies. A sophisticated result made from HTML, CSS, SVG and small amounts of JavaScript is preferable to a heavy animation/3D stack that produces the same effect.

The site should feel expensive while remaining technically economical.

## Station iteration story

The final chapters should show that product work is not only additive.

Important examples currently worth documenting:

- concepts/features can be removed even after implementation when the product becomes better without them
- STRINGS and autotune are examples of things that were removed from the evolving product
- GRAVITY returning to the clearer SIDECHAIN naming is an example of reversing a creative decision when clarity wins
- earlier visual versions of Station should be shown late in the story, after the viewer already understands the final product

The point is not to mock early work. The point is to demonstrate judgment, iteration and willingness to destroy or reverse work when that creates a better final system.

## Secondary work

SkateRoad may appear briefly as additional work near the end, with official project / itch.io links when available. It should not compete with Station for narrative space and should not be forced into Station's visual metaphor.

## Success criteria

The experience succeeds if a viewer can leave with the following impressions:

- the website itself is memorable and professionally built
- the creative concept has logic, not random effects
- Station is clearly a substantial finished product rather than a tutorial exercise
- the builder understands iteration, reduction and product judgment
- the builder can handle ordinary professional frontend work and also unusually complex browser projects
- the experience remains fast, legible and usable despite its creative presentation
